An internal combustion engine turns the chemical energy of fuel into motion. Air and fuel are drawn into a cylinder, compressed by a piston, ignited by a spark, and the expanding gas pushes the piston down. "The four strokes — intake, compression, power, exhaust — repeat thousands of times a minute." Diesel engines skip the spark and ignite fuel by compression alone, which makes them more efficient but heavier.
